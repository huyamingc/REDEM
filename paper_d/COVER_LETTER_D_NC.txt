Cover Letter — Paper D (REDEM-SSM, submission to Neurocomputing)
Date: 2026-09-11
To: The Editors, Neurocomputing (Elsevier)
Re: Submission of the manuscript "REDEM-SSM: A State-Space
Architecture with Native Online Learning, Meta-Adaptation, and
Structural Plasticity"

Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript for publication in Neurocomputing.
The paper sits at the intersection of reservoir computing,
state-space sequence models, and online / continual learning: it
asks which adaptation mechanisms must be native to a fixed
state-space host, rather than retrofitted onto a frozen feedforward
network.

We instantiate four REDEM mechanisms — an online readout (M1), a
slow-trace statistical memory (M3), structural plasticity (M4), and
a stability homeostat (M5) — on a diagonal linear recurrence with a
log-uniform timescale spectrum. The scientific object is not a new
sequence architecture, but a set of host-design requirements
isolated by a falsifying, 10-seed paired development sequence.

Key findings (all paired, sign-consistent across seeds):

1. The readout must consume the additive input path. A linear
   readout on a linearly-decayed state mixture cannot recover the
   current token (deconvolution impossibility); pooled state
   readouts fail out of sample (0/10 seeds), while the input-path
   RLS readout beats a Transformer+LoRA reference on every seed.
   Fixed multiplicative gates do not repair the state readout. We
   also report that this ranking is one of calibration rather than
   of linear information: under a clip-free sensitivity metric the
   coverage-spectrum state arms score lower, while leaving 10-12%
   of tokens on the evaluation's clip floor.
2. The state's role is statistical metadata. A fast-channel EMA
   supplies the domain statistic the gate reads, and routing over
   domain specialists retains them: forgetting improves at
   tau_m <= 1000 under both the reported and the clip-free metric.
   The pause-learning arm does not pass the paper's own retention
   rubric, and we say so: its reported stream gain reverses once
   the baseline's clip floor is removed, and its forgetting is
   never better than the baseline at tau_m in {200, 500}.
3. Routing retains specialists, and state regulation is
   conditional. Abrupt hard switching retains purer specialists
   than soft routing on forgetting, while the reported
   soft-over-abrupt stream margin reverses under the clip-free
   metric. A state-norm homeostat restores full-state statistics
   on a two-domain task but degrades on a four-domain benchmark
   and is excluded from the full stack.

On the four-domain irregular-switch benchmark the full stack
(M1+M3+M4) beats the bare host on forgetting and beats the
Transformer+LoRA reference on every seed; the result transfers to a
two-book character-level protocol. All experiments are CPU-scale
proofs of concept; we make no scaling claims. Honest negatives
(pooled state readout, fixed gates, frozen covariance, pause
learning, four-domain homeostat) are reported as design evidence,
and a dedicated metric-sensitivity section reports every headline
paired difference under both the reported and the clip-free
metric, including the cases where the sign reverses.

Why Neurocomputing. The host shares the fixed-substrate /
online-readout split of reservoir computing, while the mechanisms
are evaluated under the non-stationary streams that motivate
continual and online learning. We believe this host-requirement
framing is of direct interest to the journal's readership in
neural learning systems.
